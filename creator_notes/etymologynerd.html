01 · Platform Language
DDHqAcvOF0x · book announcement

The algorithm is now part of etymology.

The strongest update to the older etymology account is Algospeak: vocabulary no longer changes only through migration, conquest, commerce, or subculture. Platform moderation and recommendation systems create pressure fields where users invent terms, soften forbidden words, and package identity into searchable labels.

That makes the creator's book announcement more than self-promotion. It reframes the whole account: the same curiosity used to explain Latin roots or neighborhood names is now aimed at TikTok, filters, virality, and the way social media teaches people to speak around machines.

03 · Systems Of Classification
CcaawemOJ1p · B99HGTKAZA7 · CcDMVDgO48o

Scientific names are memory systems for experts.

The science posts are not merely "fun facts." Brain anatomy, infectious diseases, fish species, amino acids, geology, trees, flowers, birds, insects, butterflies, moths, and storms all depend on naming conventions that stabilize expertise across time and language.

Latin and Greek matter here because they provide a reusable construction kit: prefixes, roots, suffixes, eponyms, metaphors, locations, and visible traits become portable signs. The account works because it translates those expert systems back into ordinary curiosity.

04 · Cultural Labels
CRgpE72ADEi · CPx3luegPNt · Ctob1J5g7l3 · CpNUk7WOY3Z

The account treats culture as a naming machine.

The best cultural posts are not just about word origins; they show how domains make themselves legible. Typefaces need names so visual style can circulate. Art movements need names so a sensibility can become historical. Currencies need names so value can become official. Colors need names so perception can become communicable.

This is the broadest version of the creator's thesis: once you know how to ask where a name came from, every field becomes a museum of decisions that people stopped noticing.
