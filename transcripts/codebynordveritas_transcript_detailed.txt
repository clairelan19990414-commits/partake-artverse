Before we call it speculation, it's worth remembering. Art was never outside the market. What we now describe as volatility once operated as proto-market structures. What we call branding was already early reputation engineering. And what looks like patronage functioned as capital deployment. Take Florence, early 1500s. When the Medici Commission work, they weren't buying images. They were structuring visibility. contract specified everything, scale, subject, delivery timelines, even pigment. Ultramarine, ground from Lapis Lazuli imported from Afghanistan, was often more expensive than gold. In several renaissance contracts its use was explicitly negotiated, not for aesthetics, but as a marker of the patron's financial reach. Domenico Girlandaio's commissions, for example, included clauses on material hierarchy. And in 1508, Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel Agreement with Pope Julius II defined not just the theme, but the scope, sequence, and payment structure tied to completion. Leonardo da Vinci's Virgin of the Rocks became the subject of a legal dispute over delivery, pricing, and contractual expectations. This wasn't freedom. It was a commission-based signaling system, where artists executed and patrons encoded power through images. The Medici didn't just support art, they engineered perception. They understood that images circulate reputation faster than capital alone. So when we say that art has become financial, we're not describing a rupture, we're describing continuity. Only the language changed, the system didn't arrive, it refined itself.
