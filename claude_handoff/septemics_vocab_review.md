## INCLUDED BUT FLAGGED — Human review recommended

### Potentially sensitive register
- **masochism** (sn47) — clinical psychology term; included because it is used in
  non-clinical cultural criticism. Review if the app audience skews young.
- **suicidal** (sa25) — included only in its non-literal sense ("ruinous to one's interests").
  Example sentence makes this clear. Flag for review if literal mental health context is a concern.
- **neurotic** (sa20) — clinical origin but widely used colloquially in cultural criticism.
  Included. Review if clinical precision is required.

### Spelling ambiguity
- **idée fixe** (sn27) — French loanword with accent. Works in the vocab app but confirm
  that the write-in test mode handles the accented é gracefully (spell-check may flag it).
  Workaround: accept "idee fixe" (no accent) as a correct answer in the write-in grader.

### Near-synonym pairs that could confuse testing
- **permeate** (sv1) + **pervade** (sv16): both mean "to spread throughout." Definitions
  are differentiated (permeate = saturate from within; pervade = be present throughout),
  but multiple-choice distractors may need careful pairing to avoid ambiguity.
- **cynicism** (sn2) + **skepticism** (sn45): distinct but related. Example sentences
  draw the contrast explicitly. Keep both, but ensure test questions don't conflate them.
- **contrition** (sn13) + **remorse** (sn44): near-synonyms intentionally included
  to build precise vocabulary. The difference: contrition implies active resolve to reform;
  remorse is purely the anguished feeling. Both are useful GRE-level words.
- **compulsion** (sn3) + **inhibition** (sn41): opposites on the same psychological axis.
  Good for paired testing.
- **mastery** (sn33) + **dominance** (sn46): overlapping in meaning. Differentiated by
  context (mastery = skill; dominance = power over others). Keep both.

### Words where ZH was omitted
Chinese glosses were omitted for the following because confident translation was unclear
or the term has no clean Chinese equivalent:
- **robotism** — neologism coined by Septemics; no standard Chinese translation
- **cognoscenti** — Italian loanword; Chinese rendering varies by context
- **idée fixe** — French loanword; Chinese typically uses 固执的念头 but this is an approximation
- **automaticity** — technical psychology term; Chinese equivalent (自动性) is correct but
  may need context; included a ZH gloss anyway with a note in parentheses
